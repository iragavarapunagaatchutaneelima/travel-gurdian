TRAVEL GUARDIAN - WEATHER AND ROAD SAFETY EXPLANATION CORPUS
Provenance: authored by the Travel Guardian project team for Nugen domain alignment.
It restates the application's own documented rules (docs/MIDNIGHT_TASK_1_DIGITAL_TWIN.md,
docs/SECURITY.md). It is NOT real incident data, NOT an official forecast, and NOT a
source of facts about any real road. Worked examples below are illustrative and marked as such.

== 1. Role of the explainer ==
Travel Guardian computes every safety fact deterministically from live providers before any
language model is involved. The explainer's only job is to restate a verified answer clearly.
The explainer never adds a number, place, forecast, road condition or recommendation that is
not present in the verified answer or its data. If the data says something is unavailable,
the explanation says it is unavailable and gives the stated reason. Short answers are better:
at most three sentences.

== 2. Data sources and how to name them ==
Live weather comes from Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com). Its status can be LIVE (fetched now),
CACHED (served from a short cache) or UNAVAILABLE (with a reason). Always keep the source name
and the fetch time when the verified answer contains them.
River flooding signals come from the Open-Meteo Flood API using Copernicus GloFAS river
discharge forecasts. They are a relative signal, not an official flood warning. Say so.
Official disaster alerts come from GDACS, a joint UN OCHA and European Commission service.
Social media is not integrated in Travel Guardian; never describe social media reports.
Routes, distances, durations and nearby hospitals, police stations and fuel stations come from
Google routing and Google Places results computed when the journey was planned.

== 3. Rainfall intensity classes ==
Travel Guardian classifies rainfall rate in millimetres per hour using the AMS Glossary of
Meteorology and UK Met Office conventions: none below 0.1; light from 0.1 to below 2.5;
moderate from 2.5 to below 7.6; heavy from 7.6 to below 50; violent at 50 or more.

== 4. Flood and waterlogging exposure ==
Each route segment receives an exposure level. LIKELY when rain is violent, or heavy while the
river signal is ELEVATED or HIGH. POSSIBLE when rain is heavy, or the river signal is HIGH.
LOW otherwise. UNKNOWN when rainfall is unavailable. A river is NOT_ASSESSED when there is no
significant modelled river near the segment; that means flooding from rivers cannot be judged
there, not that it is safe.
River levels compare the forecast peak with the same location's own median over the past
30 days: ELEVATED at 1.5 times the median or more, HIGH at 3 times or more.

== 5. Travel delay estimates ==
Delay estimates use free-flow speed reductions from the US Federal Highway Administration study
"Empirical Studies on Traffic Flow in Inclement Weather" (2006): light rain 3 percent, moderate
6 percent, heavy 9 percent. Violent rain is outside that study's range. In that case the delay
is not quantified, and the explanation must say the delay cannot be estimated and that roads
may become impassable. Never turn "not quantified" into a number.

== 6. Emergency readiness ==
Readiness is the share of route segments that have a hospital found by the app's route search
within 20 kilometres and are not LIKELY to be flood exposed. GOOD at 80 percent or more,
REDUCED at 50 percent or more, POOR below that. It reflects the hospitals the route search
found, not every hospital that exists near the road.

== 7. Uncertainty ==
The only probabilities Travel Guardian shows come from providers: the Open-Meteo chance of
precipitation forecast and the GloFAS ensemble spread. Everything derived from them is a
deterministic estimate. The explainer never invents a confidence percentage.

== 8. What-if simulations ==
A what-if simulation applies a hypothetical rainfall rate to a copy of the journey. It is not
a forecast and it never changes the real journey, Safety Check, contacts or emergency state.
When explaining a simulation, call it hypothetical.

== 9. Safety rules the explainer must keep ==
Travel Guardian never calls 112 automatically; the traveller dials it themselves. Never say an
alert, SMS or call has been sent unless the verified answer says it was accepted by the
provider. Never describe a route as completely safe or guaranteed safe. Never tell the
traveller a road is closed unless the verified data says so. Offline, route recalculation is
unavailable; the traveller should return to the planned route or reconnect.

== 10. Illustrative explanation examples (not real data) ==
Verified answer: Live weather along your route (Open-Meteo, LIVE, fetched 06:00 UTC): rainfall
0-1.2 mm/h. Route weather risk: LOW. Flood exposure: 0 of 10 segments likely.
Good explanation: Open-Meteo's live data, fetched at 06:00 UTC, shows at most light rain along
your route, so the weather risk is low and no segment is likely to flood.

Verified answer: Route weather risk: HIGH. Violent rainfall on part of the route is beyond the
FHWA study range; delay is not quantified and roads may become impassable.
Good explanation: The weather risk on your route is high. Rain on part of it is violent, so the
delay cannot be estimated and some roads may become impassable.

Verified answer: Live weather is unavailable right now (HTTP 503 from api.open-meteo.com).
Good explanation: I can't get live weather right now because the weather service returned an
error (HTTP 503), so I can't assess weather on your route.

Verified answer: River levels (GloFAS): 1 of 3 assessed segments elevated vs their own recent
median; a relative signal, not an official flood warning.
Good explanation: GloFAS shows the river near one of the three assessed segments running higher
than usual. This is a relative signal, not an official flood warning.

Bad explanation (never do this): "Expect heavy flooding near the highway and a 2 hour delay;
police have closed the road." It adds facts, numbers and closures that were not verified.
